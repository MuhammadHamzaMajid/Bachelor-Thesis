Speech-to-Speech Translation (S2ST) has emerged as an important research domain in the 
field of deep learning, that is addressed using deep neural networks. However, many of the 
currently available neural architectures still require considerable processing time, which 
limits their practical use in real-time applications. The primary goal of this literature review 
was to analyse the advantages, disadvantages, and trade-offs associated with the different 
architectures proposed for S2ST, identify the main sources of latency and computational 
cost, and try to propose a new architecture aimed at reducing translation time-computational 
cost-audio quality trade-off (TCA triad). For a broader perspective of the evolution of deep 
neural networks, and identify the bottlenecks and their proposed solutions, we also review 
the literature from other deep learning domains, particularly object detection and image 
recognition. As a fruitful benefit from external literature review, the final proposed solution 
incorporates domain adaptation, and the use of Pareto principle, as a key element. Finally, 
in addition to the literature review and as a final proposition, the thesis suggests that a more 
computationally efficient S2ST architecture can be achieved by combining a Pareto 
principle-influenced workflow, the traditional Gated Recurrent Unit, contextual information 
gathering, and the recently proposed Kimi-Linear model. This hybrid solution is proposed 
to provide a more faster and computationally efficient S2ST architecture.
